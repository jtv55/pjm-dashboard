Who gets hurt first when the numbers move

For each of the 15 names: how much of the company's value is debt and how much is equity, how big a fall in enterprise value it takes to wipe out the equity, and which layer becomes the fulcrum (the layer where value runs out) beyond that. Drag the slider to stress them all at once.

Stress test

Enterprise value is market cap plus net debt. A fall in enterprise value comes from lower EBITDA, a lower multiple, or both. Debt is fixed, so equity absorbs the loss first. Solid blue is equity left, grey is net debt, striped red is debt the stressed value no longer covers (the fulcrum has moved into the debt).

        What changes the picture

          Market cap, total debt, cash, TTM EBITDA, interest coverage and free cash flow come from stockanalysis.com statistics pages read on Oct 19, 2026, and may contain errors or include lease liabilities in debt. TTM EBITDA is GAAP-based and distorted by hedge mark-to-market for the power producers, so for Vistra, NRG, Talen and Williams the reference EBITDA is the midpoint of the company's 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance (Williams sources differ, $8.3 to 8.5 billion). Constellation does not guide EBITDA; its reference is TTM. Credit ratings and covenant detail are not included unless stated. The "fulcrum" is a stylized stress measure, not a prediction of default for any company. Not investment advice.
