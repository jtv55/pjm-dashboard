PJM capacity scarcity: the case, and how it could be wrong

The Scoreboard tests small signals. This page does the other kind of work: it states one big structural idea, maps what the market already knows, lists the dated events that will prove or break it, and says in advance what would change our mind. It sizes nothing and places nothing.

Read this first. Research and education, not investment advice, and not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. The thesis and its catalyst judgments are mine and may be wrong; facts are linked to sources at the bottom. No positions are held on the strength of this page.

1. The thesis

PJM will stay short of firm power for years, and prices are held in place by policy, not by the market clearing. The open question is not whether scarcity is real, which the auctions already show, but how much of the scarcity rent generators keep once politics responds.

3. Twenty-eight years of context

Is $330 a record, or a return to normal? PJM's market monitor publishes capacity prices back to 1999 and energy prices back to 1998. The capacity market in its current form dates from 2007/08; before that there was an earlier design with different rules, so the early years are not directly comparable.
