Three ways a small account could trade what we have learned

Each idea comes from our own research, is tested on real prices where we can, and ends with the honest number: does it survive costs? Nothing here is a recommendation. It is a lab for deciding what deserves a forward test.

Read this first. Research and education, not investment advice. Options and futures can lose more than you put in. The historical results are in-sample, from small samples, and exclude many real-world frictions. We trade on paper until a forward record exists.

Strategy 1: Put the spike

After a PJM evening price spike, generator stocks tend to fall the next session (H1). The retail way to express that without shorting is a cheap out-of-the-money put. We priced it with real option trades.

Strategy 3: Buy the event, not the direction

Some catalysts are on the calendar: earnings and PJM's capacity auction results. A straddle (a call and a put at the same strike) or a strangle (a call and a put either side) pays if the stock moves a lot, whichever way. The question is whether the options already charge for it. What would make us trust any of this

  • A forward record. The cloud ledger logs the spike signal and the captured option quotes every morning, sealed in a hash-chain. It has 1 completed day and 0 signals so far; about 20 are needed to say anything.
  • Real quotes, not VWAPs. The backtests above use hour-average trade prices. Fills against the bid-ask will be worse, which is the whole story of the cost chart.
  • Two separate periods, after costs. Our kill rule: if nothing beats simple baselines after costs in two periods, we change direction. Education is the fallback.
  • Small, defined risk. Whatever is traded on paper, size it so a total loss is boring.
