AI capex to PJM capacity · v2 · as of Oct 19, 2026

How much power does PJM need for AI, and when?

This builds on the October 10 analysis of hyperscaler capex and the power gap. It keeps the chain (dollars of capex, to gigawatts, to a region), then adds what that version lacked: neocloud and lab plans, an honest look at how much of the announced load is real and lands in PJM, and PJM's own planning numbers for the auctions. The model is adjustable. Its defaults reproduce PJM's own one-year step in peak load and land within about a fifth of PJM's five-year data center growth, and the page marks which numbers are PJM's and which are guesses.

2. From dollars to gigawatts

Only chips, servers and networking draw power. The earlier analysis divides that spend by the IT cost per GW of facility power ($22B in 2025, $26B in 2026, about $29 to 30B after) and lets 30% of a year's purchases energize the next year. That gives new US data center load in nameplate GW. Bear and bull scale capex by 0.75 and 1.2.

Scope is six builders. xAI, OpenAI's own sites and other neoclouds add load, and the model includes an add-on for them below. Stargate's seven sites total about 9 GW planned, and only the Lordstown, Ohio site (under 0.3 GW) is in PJM.

3. How likely is it to need PJM?

Capex is real money, but it only becomes PJM load if the chips sit on PJM's grid and the grid can energize them. Developers apply to many places at once, so requests run far ahead of what is built. The evidence says to expect a small share of announced load to arrive.

What PJM's own forecast implies

Modo Energy counts PJM data centers at 8.2 GW operating, 41 GW under contract or construction and 71 GW earlier-stage. PJM's 2026 forecast adds 35.1 GW of large load between 2026 and 2031, and says it now counts only firm commitments. Try the conversion rates:

Why the share matters: the market monitor estimates that capacity costs tied to data center forecasts above existing load were $21.3 billion of the $47.2 billion cleared in the last three auctions (45%), and that speculative forecasts raised capacity prices by 61% against 4% for load that already exists or is committed. PJM has since lowered its near-term forecast and now vets requests harder. AEP cut its Ohio pipeline by more than half after introducing a data center tariff.

Why the market falls shorter than the planning case

The actual 2028/29 auction was 6,831 MW short, against a planning-case shortfall of 1,416 MW. The 5.4 GW difference is capacity the planning portfolio counts that did not clear: resources that did not offer, fixed-resource-requirement load handled outside the auction, and other differences. Only 525 MW of new generation and uprates cleared. This model carries that 5.4 GW forward as a "market haircut" you can change. If it holds, the December auction for 2029/30 should end roughly 8 to 9 GW short.

    6. Stranded capacity, both ways

    Stranded demand: built, but cannot be served

    Each GW of shortfall in accredited capacity is about 1/FPR GW of new load that cannot be served at PJM's reliability standard unless something else is built. The table in section 4 shows that load and the capex tied to it (a ceiling, because much of it would relocate or supply itself). PJM's answers are the backstop procurement, an interim service from June 2027 that curtails new large loads without their own capacity, and co-location rules. Each pushes load to build its own power, which is what ties up gas turbines.

    Stranded generation: built, but not needed

    The risk runs the other way if AI spending stops. Pick Bear demand and High supply above: the gap shrinks or turns into a surplus. New gas plants take about five years from order (turbine slots are reserved into 2031), so plants ordered now arrive after the next spending cycle. Long-term contracts, like the 15-year backstop contracts, are what protect a plant owner if demand falls short.

    7. What it means, and what would prove it wrong

    If the base case holds

    • PJM capacity prices stay pinned at the cap through 2029/30, and the argument over the collar is the main risk to generators after that. See the capacity desk.
    • The gap is far larger than the 9.3 GW of accredited capacity in PJM's Reliability Resource Initiative projects. Existing nuclear and gas with contracts to data centers hold value, which is what the power producers said on their calls (equity map).
    • Turbines, switchgear and transmission contractors are the bottleneck suppliers. Leverage matters for the producers that must fund builds (capital stacks).

    Checks we have set up

    • Dec 15, 2026 auction (2029/30). We expect it to clear at the cap and fall roughly 7 to 10 GW short. A result under 4 GW short, or a clear below the cap, means the haircut or the load forecast is wrong. Logged as hypothesis H6 before the result.
    • PJM's next load forecast. If it trims data center adds again, move the conversion rates down.
    • Capex guidance next quarter. A cut by any two of the six builders would move us to the bear case.
