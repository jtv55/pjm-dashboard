How much of PJM's queue will actually get built, and is it enough?

About 130 GW of projects are waiting in PJM's queue. History says most will not be built. This page estimates how much probably will, when, and what that does to the gap between PJM's demand and its reliability requirement, with the other grid operators for comparison.

What this does not know

The queue is a supply-side view. Other regions' demand forecasts, which would show whether their pipelines are enough, are not in this data. PJM's data ends at the close of 2025, before its new application cycle that opened in 2026 and will fill the near-empty 2030-plus years. Completion odds come from old queues and PJM now processes projects differently. Treat the probabilities as a base rate, not a forecast of any one project.

Sources: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, "Queued Up: 2026 Edition" project-level data (CC BY 4.0, compiled with GridTracker); EIA-860M August 2026 for planned additions and retirements; PJM and our hyperscaler capex model for demand. Method: each active request starts from the share of past requests that went on to operate once they reached its current phase, scaled by technology and size (2000 to 2015 queues). Accredited capacity uses the ELCC percentages on the power gap page. A back-test on the 2012 to 2016 queues runs about 5 points high.
