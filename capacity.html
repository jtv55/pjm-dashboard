How PJM pays power plants for being ready

Three years ahead of each delivery year, PJM runs a capacity auction. It sets a large share of what a generator earns, it has cleared at the price cap three times in a row, and the rules around it are changing right now. This page is the working notes.

How it works in a minute

Pay for readiness

PJM does not only buy energy. It pays resources to be available during the worst hours of the year. The auction price is per MW of accredited capacity per day, for a delivery year starting June 1.

Auction three years out

The Base Residual Auction (BRA) sets the price for a future year. Late additions and shortfalls are handled in later auctions and, now, in a separate backstop procurement.

A collar limits the price

Since the 2026/27 auction a cap and floor limit the result. The last three auctions all landed on the cap, which means the market wanted to clear higher.

Short of the target

The last two auctions procured less than PJM's one-event-in-ten-years reliability requirement. That is why PJM is designing a backstop and a rule for new large loads like data centers.

    What to watch, and why it matters

    The collar ends

    The cap and floor cover auctions through 2029/30. After that, prices can rise toward the $555 uncapped estimate or fall. It is the single biggest swing for generator cash flow and for customer bills, and politics will decide it.

    Backstop contracts

    The backstop procurement would sign resources for up to 15 years at a MW-weighted average of up to $555 per MW-day. On earnings calls NRG said probably less than half of its 2 GW of PJM upgrades would clear a central auction at that price, and Vistra said some projects may need more than $555.

    Large-load rules

    New data centers that do not bring their own capacity could face an interim service from mid-2027, curtailed before other load. How co-location with existing plants is treated decides who captures the value of existing nuclear and gas.

    Supply that cannot arrive

    Only 525 MW of new generation cleared. Turbine slots are reserved years ahead, and gas pipelines and transmission take three to five years. The shortfall persists unless demand slows.

    Glossary

    BRA
    Base Residual Auction. The main capacity auction, held about three years before the delivery year.
    RPM
    Reliability Pricing Model, the name of PJM's capacity market design.
    UCAP / ICAP
    Unforced capacity (what counts after accreditation) and installed capacity (nameplate). Prices are quoted per UCAP MW-day.
    Accreditation
    The share of a resource's installed capacity that PJM counts. It differs by technology and is a major lever on revenue.
    Collar
    The price cap and floor on auction results, agreed with state governors and approved by FERC.
    FRR
    Fixed Resource Requirement. Some utilities supply their own capacity outside the auction.
    RBP
    Reliability Backstop Procurement. A separate procurement of long-term capacity to cover the auction shortfall.
    IRAS
    Interim resource adequacy service for new large loads that do not bring enough capacity.
    Connect and manage
    A framework where a load or resource connects now and accepts limits when the system is tight.
    Co-location
    A data center connecting directly at a power plant, which changes who pays for the grid and capacity.
